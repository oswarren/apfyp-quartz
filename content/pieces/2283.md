![Closed charcoal boulder form in bare clay, its surface veined and splashed with pale cream markings, a hole bored through the crown with a dried grass stem in it, piece 2283 of A Penny For Your Pottery.](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0628/8434/8065/files/PXL-20260716_134547162.jpg?v=1784575327)

The most marked piece of its group, and the one whose marks have a known cause: nepheline syenite was set on top of the form while it was still closed, and water was dripped onto it. What ran off is written across the whole boulder — [[pale-veining|pale cream veining]] that wanders in thin lines through one stretch and lands as broad splashes in the next, heaviest around the crown where it started and thinning as it goes down. Under it the [[handbuilding|handbuilt]] body stays dark charcoal, granular and pitted [[bare-clay|bare clay]]. A round [[pierced-hole|hole]] is bored clean through the top, a pale patch sitting right at its mouth, and a dried grass stem rises from it in the first photo.

**What made the pale markings on 2283?** Nepheline syenite placed on the closed form and water dripped over it — the veining is where it ran. Piece 2283 of 10,000, listed at $22.83.
