Turn a piece of pottery over and you'll often find something written or stamped on the base. When it's a number, it can mean several different things depending on who made the piece and how.

**What do the numbers on the bottom of pottery mean?** Generally, a number on a pot's base is one of a few things:

- a **maker's mark or backstamp**: identifying the potter or the pottery that made it
- a **shape or mould number**: a factory's code for that particular form
- a **date code**: a stamp or cypher for the year it was made
- an **edition number**: like "12 / 50," giving the piece's place in a limited edition
- a **pattern number**: identifying the decoration rather than the form

If you're trying to identify an older or factory-made piece, it's usually one of those. On the pieces in *this* series, the number means something more specific.

## What the number means here

On A Penny For Your Pottery, the number on the base is the piece's place in a single, continuous run of 10,000 handmade pieces, and it is also the piece's price. The price of any piece is its number times one cent, so piece 2,254 is listed at $22.54. Turn the piece over and you're reading both its position in the series and its price tag at once.

It isn't a mould number (each piece is [[handbuilding|handbuilt]], one at a time), and it isn't an edition number in the usual sense: there's exactly one piece with each number, made once, at that point in the run.

## Three ways the number is written

The number is applied by hand, and the pieces documented so far show three styles:

- **Hand-painted**: brushed on in dark pigment, as on [[2253]], the last of an early painted run
- **Incised**: carved directly into the clay, as on [[2254]], where the style first switches
- **Handwritten**: written on the bare base in a medium the photos don't identify, as on [[2085]]

In the 2250–2260 batch the switch from painted to incised happens right between pieces 2253 and 2254. ## A few more questions

**Is the number a signature?** Not exactly. A signature says who made a piece; this number says *which* piece it is (its one place in the 10,000) and what it's priced at.

**Does every piece have one?** Yes. The number is the piece's identity in the series, so every piece carries it on the base.

**What does the number tell me about the price?** Everything: the price is the number times one cent, and it never goes on sale. A higher number is a later piece and a higher price.
