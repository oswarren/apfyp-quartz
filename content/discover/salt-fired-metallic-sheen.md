Scattered through this series are pieces that catch the light like brushed metal: a pewter-grey shine across a dark, rough clay body. There's no metal in them, and they aren't glazed to look that way. The shine comes from how they were fired.

**Why does this pottery look metallic?** The sheen is a salt-firing effect. In a salt firing, common salt is introduced into the kiln at high temperature; it vaporizes and settles on the pots, and the sodium bonds with the bare clay to leave a thin, glassy, pewter-like luster, with no applied glaze involved. On these pieces the shine sits directly on the raw surface, catching every raised ridge and torn edge.

## What salt firing is

Salt firing is a traditional high-temperature technique. Once the kiln reaches its top heat, salt is added to the chamber, where it vaporizes and reacts with the silica in the clay itself. Instead of a glaze brushed on before firing, the surface forms *during* the firing, out of the clay and the salt vapor together. The result depends on the clay body and where a piece sits in the kiln, which is why the effect varies from a faint grey flash to an allover metallic crust.

On the [[metallic-sheen|metallic-sheen pieces]] in this series, that reaction is what the luster is: the salt firing flashed the bare clay, and the raised surface reads like brushed pewter. Where a piece carries other surfaces too (a pooled glassy band at a rim, say), each [[metallic-sheen|piece page]] keeps to what its own photos show and names the salt firing only for the sheen.

## A few more questions

**Is there real metal in the clay?** No. The look is a fired surface effect, not a metal content or coating. It's clay that came out of a salt firing shining like pewter.

**Is it a metallic glaze?** No applied glaze. That's the distinction. A metallic glaze is painted on before firing; here the surface forms from the clay and salt vapor during the firing itself.

**Why isn't every piece like this?** Most of the series isn't salt-fired, and salt firing doesn't flash every piece the same way. The sheen is one look among many across the 10,000-piece run, which is part of why the pieces that have it stand out.
